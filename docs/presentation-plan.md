# November 11 discussion plan

## Audience and purpose

PDRI/DevLab academic audience. The working allocation is a 30–35 minute research presentation within a one-hour session, leaving room for discussion. Present the measurement contribution, a transparent descriptive snapshot, and the design needed to answer substantive political questions.

## Narrative

1. Why an incumbent's political advantage, public legitimacy, and favorable language can diverge.
2. The selected article/action/government unit and the three coding dimensions.
3. A synthetic contrast that holds the reported action constant while changing narrator evaluation.
4. Corpus selection, version boundaries, and the fixed descriptive window.
5. Exact label distributions and changing monthly coverage.
6. What the descriptive patterns suggest, and the competing explanations they leave open.
7. Validation and political research designs for ownership, regulation, and citizen attitudes.
8. Concrete questions for the audience about the reputational construct and evidence needed for stronger claims.

The deck includes speaker notes and appendix material. Synthetic examples teach the codebook and must never be introduced as observed country cases.

## Preparation priorities

| Work | Intended use | Completion criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Literature synthesis | Situate media control and government portrayal, especially beyond the US | Verified references and explicit comparison with this measurement |
| Blinded semantic review | Establish where the dimensions work and fail | Independent judgments before reconciliation, per-field error accounting |
| Joint label tables | Test whether different dimensions diverge within articles | Counts include nulls and prompt/version metadata |
| Country/source composition analysis | Assess robustness of pooled descriptive patterns | Comparable denominators, stable panels, weighting sensitivity |
| Historical entity context | Distinguish national incumbents and institutional roles over time | Versioned crosswalk with dates, ambiguity flags and held-out checks |
| Real article illustrations | Explain successful and failed coding | Source access and translation checked; publication rights reviewed |

Use November 2 as a working synthesis checkpoint, not a claim of guaranteed corpus completion. Freeze the talk's final data version before rehearsal and record any changes from this October 29 package. The present deck is already usable for a methods-and-preliminary-results talk if later analyses remain unfinished.

## Questions to invite

- Is the reasonable-observer reputational construct useful, and which disagreements should remain visible?
- What would separate selection of unfavorable events from evaluative narration about the same events?
- Which ownership or regulatory changes offer credible within-context comparisons?
- What audience exposure and attitude data would make an inference about political consequences possible?

## Publication boundary

Keep public materials in this repository's defined directories. Do not copy the operational project root, private messages, raw articles, provider ledgers, credentials, or database dumps. Any expansion from aggregate reproduction to source-level data is a separate release with its own documentation.
